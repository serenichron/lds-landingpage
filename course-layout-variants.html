Lesson 1 of 7

How much design work do online programmes really require?

Most institutions estimate the effort like a content-development project. Effective online learning is something larger.

By the end of this lesson — you'll be able to estimate the design effort required for an online programme in your own institutional context, and justify that estimate to stakeholders.
Before
Think about a recent online programme launch you know about. What was the original timeline? What was the actual one? Keep that example in mind as you continue.
During

What is often assumed

Most institutions plan for content development. But effective online learning requires something much more substantial: a fully designed course architecture.

What is actually required

That architecture typically includes learning outcomes mapped to activities, assessments aligned to outcomes, multimedia production, accessibility compliance, platform configuration, and quality-assurance testing. This is not simply content creation — it is end-to-end design and development.

3–5× the effort of preparing equivalent face-to-face teaching, for a single online module. For a full programme, multiply across every module.

That ratio surprises most institutional planners. It is the most consistent finding across the dozen institutions Andrew has worked with on first-cohort builds. The teams that estimate accurately at the start are the ones that ship on time.

"If your project plan reads like a content-creation brief, you are already six months behind." — Andrew Doig
